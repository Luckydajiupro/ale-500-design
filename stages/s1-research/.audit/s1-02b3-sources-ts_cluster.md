## A. Primary sources used by the nine rows

1. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.533 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 121.533 - Responsibility for operational control: Domestic operations." — 「The pilot in command and the aircraft dispatcher are jointly responsible for the preflight planning, delay, and dispatch release of a flight in compliance with this chapter and operations specifications.」 (b); (c) assigns the dispatcher responsibility for monitoring the progress of each flight, issuing information necessary for its safety, and cancelling or redispatching a flight. → ts_avi_wf01 primary.
2. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.617 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 121.617 - Alternate airport for departure." — If takeoff weather is below the landing minimums in the certificate holder's operations specifications, the dispatch or flight release must specify an alternate airport within one hour (two-engine) or two hours (three or more engines) of the departure airport at normal cruising speed in still air with one engine inoperative; each required alternate must be listed in the dispatch or flight release. → ts_avi_wf01.
3. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.639 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 121.639 - Fuel supply: All domestic operations." — No person may dispatch or take off an airplane unless it has enough fuel to fly to the airport to which it is dispatched, then to the most distant alternate required, and then for 45 minutes at normal cruising fuel consumption (30 minutes for day VFR, nontransport-category airplanes certificated after December 31, 1964). → ts_avi_wf01.
4. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.657 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 121.657 - Flight altitude rules." — Day VFR not below 1,000 ft above the surface or 1,000 ft from any obstruction; night VFR and IFR not below 1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within 5 miles of the intended course, and 2,000 ft in designated mountainous areas. → ts_avi_wf01.
5. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.593 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 121.593 - Dispatching authority: Domestic operations." — 「no person may start a flight unless an aircraft dispatcher specifically authorizes that flight」 (exception: landing at an intermediate airport named in the original release and remaining not more than one hour). → ts_avi_wf01.
6. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/5.53 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 5.53 - System analysis and hazard identification." — The certificate holder must analyse the systems identified under §5.51 and use that analysis to identify hazards; the analysis must consider (1) function and purpose, (2) the operating environment, (3) the outline of processes and procedures, (4) personnel, equipment and facilities, and (5) interfaces with other systems, and the holder must 「develop and maintain processes to identify hazards in the context of the system analysis」 (c). → ts_avi_wf02 primary.
7. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/5.55 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR § 5.55 - Safety risk assessment and control." — Processes must analyse the safety risk of hazards identified under §5.53(c); 「Define a process for conducting risk assessment that allows for the determination of acceptable safety risk.」 (b); processes must develop safety risk controls (c) and 「Evaluate whether the risk will be acceptable with the proposed safety risk control applied before the safety risk control is implemented.」 (d). → ts_avi_wf02.
8. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/part-5 — HTTP 200 — "14 CFR Part 5 - SAFETY MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS" — Part 5 Subpart C (Safety Risk Management, §§5.51-5.57) and Subpart D (Safety Assurance, §§5.71-5.75); source Doc. FAA-2009-0671, Amdt. 5-1, 80 FR 1326 (Jan. 8, 2015). → ts_avi_wf02 (part-level structure).
9. https://www.en.droneregler.dk/commercial-drone-operations/overview-of-drone-operations-in-the-specific-category/sora-risk-assessment-for-the-specific-category — HTTP 200 — "SORA - Risk assessment for the specific category" (Danish Civil Aviation and Railway Authority / Trafikstyrelsen) — The page sets out the ten SORA steps: ConOps (step 1), initial ground risk (step 2), final ground risk with mitigations M1 strategic / M2 systems such as a parachute / M3 emergency response plan (step 3), initial air risk ARC-a to ARC-d and residual ARC (steps 4-5), tactical mitigation performance requirements (step 6), SAIL determination from final GRC and final ARC (step 7), the 24 OSOs with integrity and assurance levels (step 8), adjacent airspace and fly-away handling (step 9), and compilation of the safety portfolio for an operational authorisation (step 10); the application must present all ten steps as one document together with the operations manual. → ts_avi_wf03 primary.
10. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/176.83 — HTTP 200 — "49 CFR § 176.83 - Segregation." — Segregation is by distances or by steel bulkheads/decks; the General Segregation Table defines terms 1 "Away from", 2 "Separated from", 3 "Separated by a complete compartment or hold from" and 4 "Separated longitudinally by an intervening complete compartment or hold from"; "Away from" requires a minimum horizontal separation of 3 m (10 ft); "Separated from" requires different compartments or holds or, on deck, at least 6 m (20 ft); "Separated by a complete compartment or hold from" requires at least 12 m (39 ft) on deck; the longitudinal term requires at least 24 m (79 ft); two hazardous materials requiring any segregation may not be transported in the same cargo transport unit (d); one container space is 6 m fore-and-aft by 2.5 m athwartship (f)(4). → ts_mar_wf01 primary.
11. https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Safety/Pages/DangerousGoods-default.aspx — HTTP 200 — "The International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) Code" — SOLAS chapter VII makes the IMDG Code mandatory for the carriage of packaged dangerous goods; the Code covers packing, container traffic and stowage 「with particular reference to the segregation of incompatible substances」; adopted in 1965 as recommendatory and made mandatory by resolution A.716(17) from 1 January 2004, amended on a two-year cycle. → ts_mar_wf01 secondary.
12. https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/safety/pages/css-code.aspx — HTTP 200 — "Code of Safe Practice for Cargo Stowage and Securing (CSS Code)" — Adopted in November 1991 by resolution A.714(17); its purpose is to provide an international standard for safe stowage and securing; the General Principles state that 「All cargoes should be stowed and secured in such a way that the ship and persons on board are not put at risk.」 and that 「The safe stowage and securing of cargoes depend on proper planning, execution and supervision.」; it also states that 「Personnel planning and supervising the stowage and securing of cargo should have a sound practical knowledge of the application and content of the Cargo Securing Manual.」 and that 「the Master is responsible for the safe conduct of the voyage and the safety of the ship, its crew and cargo」. → ts_mar_wf02 primary.
13. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/160.206 — HTTP 200 — "33 CFR § 160.206 - Information required in an NOA." — Each Notice of Arrival must contain the items in Table 160.206: vessel information (name, registered owner, country of registry, call sign, IMO number, operator, charterer, classification society, MMSI, VRP control number), voyage information (last five foreign ports and dates, receiving facility/port, ETA, ETD, current position, 24-hour contact), cargo information (general description; name/UN number and amount of certain dangerous cargo), crew and persons on board (name, date of birth, nationality, passport or mariner's document number, duties, embarkation port), equipment condition, the ISM Code notice and the ISPS Code notice. → ts_mar_wf03 primary.
14. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/160.204 — HTTP 200 — "33 CFR § 160.204 - Exemptions and exceptions." — A vessel of less than 500 gross tons need not submit the ISM Code Notice (entry 7 of Table 160.206) and a U.S. vessel need not submit the ISPS Code Notice information (entry 8); ferries operating on a fixed schedule must file the schedule with the Captain of the Port at least 24 hours before the first arrival. → ts_mar_wf03.
15. https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Safety/Pages/Pilotage.aspx — HTTP 200 — "Pilotage" — Pilots with local knowledge guide vessels into and out of port; IMO recognised the importance of pilotage in 1968 through Assembly resolution A.159(ES.IV), which recommends that Governments organise pilotage services and define the ships and classes of ships for which pilotage is necessary; resolution A.960(23) (2003) gives recommendations on training, certification and operational procedures for maritime pilots; pilot transfer arrangements are regulated by SOLAS regulation V/23. → ts_mar_wf03.
16. https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Safety/Pages/VesselTrafficServices.aspx — HTTP 200 — "Vessel Traffic Services" — VTS are shore-side systems ranging from information messages to extensive traffic management within a port or waterway; SOLAS regulation V/12(1) states that 「Vessel traffic services (VTS) contribute to safety of life at sea, safety and efficiency of navigation, and protection of the marine environment」; the IMO guidelines are resolution A.1158(32) (2021, revising A.857(20)). → ts_mar_wf03.
17. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.36 — HTTP 200 — "29 CFR § 1910.36 - Design and construction requirements for exit routes." — 「At least two exit routes must be available in a workplace to permit prompt evacuation of employees and other building occupants during an emergency」 (b)(1); exit route doors must be openable from the inside without keys, tools or special knowledge (d)(1); exit route capacity must support the maximum permitted occupant load and may not decrease toward the point of discharge (f)(1)-(2); exit access must be at least 28 inches (71.1 cm) wide (g)(2) with a ceiling of at least 7 ft 6 in (2.3 m) (g)(1). → ts_fir_wf01 primary.
18. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.37 — HTTP 200 — "29 CFR § 1910.37 - Maintenance, safeguards, and operational features for exit routes." — Exit routes must be arranged so that employees are not required to travel toward a high hazard area unless adequately shielded (a)(2), kept free and unobstructed with no dead-end corridors and no passage through a room that can be locked (a)(3); safeguards such as sprinklers, alarms, fire doors and exit lighting must be in proper working order (a)(4); exits must be marked by a sign reading "Exit" illuminated to at least 5 foot-candles (54 lux) with letters at least 6 in (15.2 cm) high (b)(2), (b)(6), (b)(7). → ts_fir_wf01.
19. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.106 — HTTP 200 — "29 CFR § 1910.106 - Flammable liquids." — A flammable liquid is one with a flashpoint at or below 199.4 °F (93 °C), classified into Categories 1-4 (a)(19); 「The distance between any two flammable liquid storage tanks shall not be less than 3 feet」 and not less than one-sixth the sum of their diameters (b)(2)(ii); normal venting must be sized in accordance with API 2000 or at least the filling/withdrawal rate (b)(2)(iv); emergency relief venting for fire exposure follows Table H-10 with the formula CFH = 1,107·A^0.82 (b)(2)(v); drainage or diked areas must prevent discharge from reaching waterways and dike capacity must not be smaller than the largest tank (b)(2)(vii). → ts_fir_wf02 primary.
20. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.159 — HTTP 200 — "29 CFR § 1910.159 - Automatic sprinkler systems." — Designs must provide the discharge patterns, densities and water flow characteristics necessary for complete coverage (c)(1)(i); acceptance tests on systems installed after January 1, 1981 must be conducted and recorded, covering flushing of underground connections, hydrostatic tests of piping, air tests of dry-pipe systems, dry-pipe valve operation and drainage facilities (c)(3); at least one automatic water supply must be capable of the design water flow for at least 30 minutes (c)(4); systems with more than 20 sprinklers need a local waterflow alarm (c)(9); minimum vertical clearance from deflectors is 18 in (45.7 cm) (c)(10); hydraulically designed systems must be identified with the location, number of sprinklers and basis of design (c)(11). → ts_fir_wf03 primary.
